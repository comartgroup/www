Control points

Five checkpoints,
and what each one is for.

IQC
Incoming quality control — material and components verified before they enter production
FAI
First article inspection — the first output of a setup checked against the approved specification
IPQC
In-process quality control — sampling and control points during the run, not only at the end
FQC
Final quality control — finished product checked against functional and cosmetic criteria
OQC
Outgoing quality control — packed goods verified before shipment leaves the plant

Testing capability

What gets tested is agreed during engineering, not invented at the end of the project. Functional, reliability and environmental testing are defined against the product's actual use scenario and the requirements of your destination market.

For mounts and holders that means the failure modes those products actually have: clamp force after repeated cycling, ball joint and hinge retention, adhesive and suction performance after temperature cycling, and finish durability on the surfaces a user handles every day. A part that passes on day one and loosens by week three is what this testing exists to catch.

  • Functional testingAgainst the criteria defined in the test plan during engineering.
  • Reliability testingLife, cycle and stress testing appropriate to the product's use.
  • Environmental testingTemperature, humidity and other conditions relevant to the market.
  • Material & dimensional testingVerified in our own laboratory rather than sent out by default.
  • Third-party laboratoriesAccredited testing coordinated by COMART where certification requires it.
