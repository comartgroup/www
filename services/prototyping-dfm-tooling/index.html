Prototyping, DFM & Tooling

The stage where a phone holder stops being a design and becomes a steel tool — and where changes stop being cheap.

Stage 03 of 6 Concept → Mass Production
Overview

We build prototypes to answer specific questions, not to produce a nice-looking sample. An appearance model validates form and finish. A functional model validates the mechanism — whether a clamp holds a large phone on a rough road, whether a hinge survives its cycle count, whether a stand tips when you tap the screen.

DFM review happens before mold design, not after T1. On a car mount that usually means arguing about draft on a visible face, where the parting line falls on a curved housing, and whether a snap fit can be molded without a side action that adds cost to every shot for the life of the tool.

Mold design, flow, parting, gating and ejection are planned as one decision set, because changing one of them later changes all of them. A gate moved to fix a cosmetic defect can put a weld line somewhere structural.

More than 318 proprietary molds are running in our plants. Mold design, trial, revision and acceptance stay under our own process control rather than being subcontracted and re-quoted.

What this stage covers

  • Appearance & functional prototypesBuilt to answer a defined question — form, mechanism, fit, or failure mode.
  • DFM / DFMA reviewManufacturability and assembly review before mold design starts, when a change still costs a drawing revision rather than a tool modification.
  • Mold designTool structure planned against the production machine, cycle and volume it has to serve.
  • Mold flow, parting, gating & ejectionPlanned together, because each constrains the others and every one of them shows on the visible surface of a consumer product.
  • Cosmetic tooling decisionsParting line placement, texture, and ejector positions chosen around the faces a customer will see on a holder, mount or charging pad.
  • T1 trial & mold revisionFirst shots, dimensional and functional review, and a documented revision loop.
  • Mold acceptance & transferFormal acceptance criteria, then transfer into stable production on the machine the tool was designed for.
