Product Planning & Design

Before tooling is committed, we turn a car mount, phone holder or charging product idea into a defined, buildable and costed proposal.

Stage 01 of 6 Concept → Mass Production
Overview

Most projects that go wrong in tooling went wrong at definition. We start by understanding who uses the product, in what situation, and what it actually has to do — then work back to structure, material and cost.

For the products we build most often, that questioning gets specific quickly. A car mount for a delivery driver who mounts and removes a phone forty times a shift is a different product from one for a commuter who docks once a day, even when the brief for both says "car mount". One needs a one-handed release and a clamp that tolerates abuse; the other can prioritize a cleaner profile on the dashboard. Which one you are building changes the mechanism, the material and the tooling cost — so it is worth settling before anyone draws anything.

The output of this stage is a product definition your team can approve and our engineers can build against. More than 100 granted patents have come out of this work, from our own product and mechanical development rather than from licensing someone else's.

Where this matters most

  • Car mounts & phone holdersMounting method, release mechanism, device size range and the dashboard or vent interface — decisions that set the whole tooling strategy.
  • Wireless charging productsCoil position, thermal path and the housing around them, planned together rather than fitted afterwards.
  • Stands & device accessoriesStability, adjustment range and the finish the product is judged on at retail.

What this stage covers

  • User scenario & requirement definitionWho uses the product, in what environment, and the functional requirements that follow from it.
  • Product planningFeature set, model line-up, positioning against your existing range and target market.
  • Industrial designAppearance, form and usability, developed with molding constraints already in view rather than discovered at DFM.
  • Preliminary material & process adviceMaterial families and processes that fit the requirement, the finish and the volume.
  • Target cost & volume feasibilityWhether the concept can be built at your target cost and volume before tooling is committed.
